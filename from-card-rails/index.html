Background

Why card rails transfer

Concept prototype — illustrative data

A payment rail is a machine for answering three questions at speed: is the credential real, is the person real, and who eats the loss when the answer was wrong. Everything else — the messaging formats, the settlement windows, the fee tables — is implementation detail around those three. Stablecoins replace the implementation and inherit the questions unanswered. That’s why the background transfers: I’ve spent the last year inside Visa’s 3DS platform working on the answered versions.

Credential. Network tokenization replaces a card number with a vaulted token, bound to a device and validated with a cryptogram — so a stolen credential is a dead credential. To say it plainly, because the words collide: this is payment-credential tokenization, not on-chain asset tokenization; the latter is adjacent knowledge for me, not hands-on experience. On the new rail, the credential question becomes key management — a signature proves the key, never the holder — and the tokenization playbook (bind the credential to something, make theft worthless) is exactly the playbook that’s missing.

Person. EMV 3DS exists to let an issuer challenge a human, and Visa Payment Passkey moves that challenge onto FIDO/WebAuthn — authentication that travels with the person, not the plastic. Stablecoin rails have no identity layer at all; the hollow dot in the diagram above is the largest product opportunity on the page, and passkeys are the obvious import.

Loss. Card rails allocate loss by rulebook — chargebacks, and the 3DS liability shift that moves fraud loss to whoever authenticated. Loss allocation is an incentive engine: when liability moved, authentication rates followed. Parity’s dispute surface is that engine redesigned for an irreversible rail.

Economics. Enriched authorization data is the proof that merchants change behaviour when the price is right: richer data in exchange for interchange relief, priced in basis points. Acceptance economics is the discipline of knowing what each basis point buys — which is the entire method behind CryptoPos and Parity’s cost console.

Thirty years of card infrastructure is, in the end, a library of answers to exactly the questions stablecoin products are now discovering. I’d rather port the answers than rediscover them.

Public product names and open standards only: Visa Payment Passkey, Visa TMS, EMV 3DS, FIDO/WebAuthn.
