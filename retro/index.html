Build journal

What I got wrong

Kept on purpose, in one place. Every POC on this site made at least one mistake worth admitting — and each one changed the design that shipped in the prototype.

Parity

  • I benchmarked against headline interchange. Wrong benchmark — interchange buys fraud screening, authentication, disputes and a funds guarantee. Pricing the buy-back shrank the gap and made the model honest.
  • I treated “hold” as the cheap path. Holding is a credit position against the issuer plus depeg exposure — the treasury surface exists because the settlement console was quietly lying.
  • I assumed the speed story was the chain’s to win. Redemption ends on a wire or ACH leg; the durable advantages are finality and programmability, not speed — a weaker pitch and a truer one.

DeFiLend

  • Closed all five gaps at once — a demo flex, not a roadmap; a funded version ships the simulator first and makes everything else earn its slot.
  • Automation before trust — AI strategies ask users to delegate money before they’ve practised moving it; that inverts my own belief.
  • Wrapped stocks hand-wave the hard part — the contract is a weekend; securities law and broker integrations are the actual product.
  • Insurance priced as a feature, not a cost — premiums eat yield, and I never modelled willingness to pay.

CryptoPos

  • Led with asset breadth — four assets read as ambition; stablecoin-first is the honest architecture.
  • Rewards ignored who pays — card-style 2% without card-style interchange to fund it; the margin floor exists because v1 let a demo merchant lose money on every sale.
  • Refunds were an afterthought — pinning value at sale decides who eats the price move; I should have recognised a loss-allocation question on sight.

Coinbase, re-read

  • My first framing said “Coinbase doesn’t have derivatives” — factually wrong (US futures since ~2023). Checking the claim reshaped the POC around experience gaps, and made it stronger. Check the claim before you design the fix.
  • Treated prediction markets as a feature — event contracts are a licensing and compliance program with a UI on top.
  • Portfolio analysis assumed data users don’t have — cost basis across wallets and venues; it demos beautifully on demo data, which is exactly the trap.

NFT lending

  • Started with the gallery — a beautiful grid with a borrow button, for a product whose actual product is an oracle.
  • Version one used the raw floor price — one wash trade moved every loan ceiling in the collection; red-teaming my own oracle should have been step one.
  • Modelled liquidity as a constant — the bids that absorb liquidations are correlated with the crash that triggers them.
