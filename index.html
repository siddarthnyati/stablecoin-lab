Stablecoins are a rails change, not a problem change.

Whoever moves money still has to answer the three questions card networks spent thirty years on: is this credential real, is this person real, and who eats the loss when it isn’t. I spent the last year on exactly those questions inside Visa’s 3DS platform — network tokenization, passkey authentication, enriched authorization data, merchant acceptance economics. These are the same questions, asked on the other side of the rail. The tiles below are the evidence.

A point of view

People don’t adopt financial products they can’t practise first.

It surfaced independently in two of these POCs — a market simulator with virtual assets in DeFiLend, paper trading in the Coinbase concept — before I noticed it was the same idea twice. Cards solved practice with reversibility: you can try, because someone will make you whole. Crypto rails are irreversible, so practice has to be designed in deliberately. That’s a belief I’d bring to a stablecoin roadmap, not a coincidence.
