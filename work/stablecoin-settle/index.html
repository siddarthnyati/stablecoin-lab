Parity

I spend my days on the layer of card rails that decides who’s real and who pays when they aren’t. This is that experience, pointed at stablecoins — one problem chosen, one fix proposed, six calculators to argue with.

The why, in one breath

A card payment quietly answers three questions — is the card real, is the person real, who pays when it goes wrong. A stablecoin payment answers almost none of them. Parity takes the biggest gap — refunds and disputes — designs three fixes, and prices everything with calculators you can play with.

What I do at Visa

3-D Secure platform: Visa Payment Passkey (FIDO/WebAuthn), network tokens, enriched authorization data, and the economics that make merchants say yes.

Why it transfers

Every rail must answer the same three questions — credential, person, loss. Card rails answer them so quietly merchants forget they’re questions.

The problem I chose

Chargebacks are the feature crypto forgot to copy. On an irreversible rail there is no dispute process — so I designed three, and priced them.

The POC · featured problem

Chargebacks are the feature crypto forgot to copy.

On card rails, a cardholder who gets a brick instead of a laptop has a process — and if the transaction was 3DS-authenticated, the fraud loss has already shifted to the issuer by design. On a stablecoin rail, a refund is just a new payment the merchant may or may not send. So the POC proposes three dispute mechanisms and prices each one.

The pattern worth porting is liability shift as an incentive: whoever attests — to delivery, identity, or escrow release — carries the loss when the attestation is wrong. Loss allocation is the product; everything else is plumbing.

“Fully reserved” guarantees that at one instant, reserves ≥ tokens. It does not guarantee an audit, anything between attestation dates, or redemption at par, at speed, in stress. Holding the coin is credit exposure — card rails never ask a merchant to underwrite their acquirer.

“Convert to fiat” is one button and five parties. Illustrative timing.

Supply outstanding: 1,000,000 USDC (demo)
  1. Merchant sends 50,000 USDC to the issuer’s redemption address
  2. Chain transfer finalises in seconds
  3. Issuer burns the tokens; supply drops, the claim is extinguished
  4. Issuer → bank instructs release of $50,000 from reserves
  5. Banking rail wire or ACH carries the fiat; this is where the clock lives
  6. Merchant bank funds land, same-day to T+1 (demo) — card speed, because the last leg is the same rail
