POC 1 · Aave, reimagined

DeFiLend

Borrowing isn’t broken — it’s biased. DeFi fixes the bias, then scares away everyone it was fixing it for.

Premise

2021: a Dartmouth admit with a scholarship, and the bank still wanted our home — worth 5× the loan — at 12% APR, while my father’s blue-chip portfolio counted for nothing. The barrier to credit isn’t money; it’s rules about what counts.

Why it’s hard

Aave answers the fairness problem — collateral in, loan out, no committee. But the user it matters for has never held a health factor in their head, and one irreversible mistake is a permanent goodbye.

The insight

Exploring Aave’s retail surface, five gaps — and none are protocol problems. Every one closes with product.

Five gaps → five features

Closed in order of fear.

Confidence→ Market simulator, virtual assets rehearse a liquidation with nothing at stake · converts visitors to depositors
Onboarding→ On-demand Learn Mode tooltips at the moment of fear · cuts first-session drop-off
Protection→ Insurance layer at deposit a checkbox in the flow, not a protocol to research · licence to make mistakes
Automation→ AI strategies by risk appetite idle assets get a default job · fewer errors and liquidations
Community→ Vaults · contests · leaderboards “Stable Yield”, “Web3 Innovation”, “RWA Income” · curation beats decision fatigue

Growth levers · each with its meter

Stock & RWA→ Borrow against wrapped stocks the Dartmouth loan, fixed · tracks stock-collateral TVL, new-to-crypto users
NFT credit→ NFT collateral · its own POC tracks floor-price collateralisation, loan volume, default rates
AI assistant→ Natural-language lending guide tracks activation, reduction in user errors & liquidations
Card rewards→ Cashback aggregated into deposits tracks reward inflows, % converted to deposits

North star: simulator → first real deposit. ★

TVL is the vanity metric here — mercenary capital follows incentives out the door, and one whale moves it more than a thousand newcomers. A practised newcomer committing real funds is the whole thesis, proved one user at a time.

What I got wrong · what’s next

What I got wrong

  • Closed all five gaps at once — a demo flex, not a roadmap; a funded version ships the simulator first and makes everything else earn its slot.
  • Automation before trust — AI strategies ask users to delegate money before they’ve practised moving it; that inverts my own belief.
  • Wrapped stocks hand-wave the hard part — the contract is a weekend; securities law and broker integrations are the actual product.
  • Insurance priced as a feature, not a cost — premiums eat yield, and I never modelled willingness to pay.

Next: strip to the simulator-to-deposit funnel, test the north-star conversion with real new-to-crypto users, and publish the avoided-liquidations number even when it’s unflattering.
