Premise

In 2021 I was admitted to Dartmouth with a scholarship. The bank’s price for the remaining loan: our home as collateral — worth five times the loan — at 12% APR. My father’s portfolio of blue-chip and bank stocks was worth more than the loan itself; it counted for nothing. To the system, we looked risky. Borrowing isn’t broken — it’s biased: the biggest barrier to credit isn’t money, it’s rules about what counts.

Aave is the counter-proposal — deposit collateral, borrow against it, no W-2, no committee. Global, always on, transparent, composable. And almost everyone who could use it bounces off it. The protocol answered the fairness problem and left the adoption problem entirely open.

Why it’s hard

The user this matters for has never held a health factor in their head. The stakes are irreversible, the vocabulary is hostile, and one early mistake is a permanent goodbye — there is no practice mode, no one to call, and no way back. Products for the risk-averse can’t just explain risk; they have to let people rehearse it.

The insight

Exploring Aave’s retail surface, the gaps clustered into five — and none of them are protocol problems. Onboarding & education: no in-app tutorials or contextual nudges for a first session that decides everything. Confidence: no paper trading — users fear losing funds and have no way to practise. Protection: no integration with decentralised insurance. Automation: no risk-tiered strategies — new users don’t know what idle assets are for. Community: no social layer at all. Every one closes with product.

What was built

Five gaps, closed in order of fear.

  1. Market simulator with virtual assets — the practice-first belief, literal: rehearse supplying, borrowing and a liquidation with nothing at stake before the first real dollar moves.
  2. On-demand Learn Mode — contextual tooltips that appear when summoned, not a tutorial gauntlet before the product. Education at the moment of fear, not before it.
  3. Insurance layer — decentralised cover integrated at deposit time, so protection is a checkbox in the flow, not a separate protocol to research.
  4. AI portfolio strategies — pick a risk appetite, get a rebalancing strategy; idle assets get a default job. Automation for people who don’t yet know what to automate.
  5. Pre-vetted community vaults — “Stable Yield”, “Web3 Innovation”, “RWA Income” — curation against decision fatigue, plus contests and leaderboards for the crypto-native crowd.
  6. Stock-backed loans — borrow against brokerage and wrapped-stock collateral, with RWA and real-estate tokens extending the base. This is the Dartmouth loan, fixed: the portfolio finally counts.

Success criteria

TVL is the vanity metric here.

TVL is the number every DeFi deck leads with, and it’s the wrong one: it pools mercenary capital that arrives for an incentive and leaves with it, and one whale can move it more than a thousand new users. The point of DeFiLend is the thousand users. The north star follows the practice-first belief all the way through the funnel.

What I got wrong · what’s next

What I got wrong

  • I closed all five gaps at once. The prototype ships insurance, education, simulation, AI, social and new collateral simultaneously — a demo flex, not a roadmap. A funded version sequences by activation impact: simulator first, education inside it, everything else earns its slot with data.
  • The automation asks for trust the product hasn’t earned yet. AI strategies assume a user who’ll delegate money before they’ve practised moving it themselves. That inverts my own belief — practice builds trust, then automation spends it. Order matters and I had it backwards.
  • Wrapped-stock collateral hand-waves the hard part. The smart contract is a weekend; the securities-law, custody and broker-integration reality is the actual product. The prototype presents as solved something I’d scope as a multi-quarter partnership program.
  • Insurance is priced as a feature, not a cost. Premiums come out of yield, and I never modelled what protection a fearful user will actually pay for. The attach-rate metric exists because I couldn’t answer that question honestly.

What I’d do next: strip the POC to the simulator-to-first-deposit funnel and test the north star conversion with real new-to-crypto users; take the stock-collateral thesis to a broker partnership conversation with the legal scoping done first; and publish the avoided-liquidations metric even when it’s unflattering — it’s the number that makes this product different.
