NFT lending

Lend against an asset with no reliable price, and the interface is the least of your problems.

Premise

An NFT holder is asset-rich and liquidity-poor — crypto’s version of the borrower whose portfolio doesn’t count. Borrowing against the asset unlocks capital without a sale.

Why it’s hard

No reliable price — the “floor” is one thin order from fiction. Liquidity vanishes exactly when liquidations cluster. And the keenest borrowers know their valuation is generous.

The insight

When you can’t price the collateral, price the exit: what will it fetch, how fast, in a bad week? Card rails call this thin-file underwriting.

The underwriting design

A lending book that admits what it doesn’t know.

Oracle→ Trimmed median of realised sales raw floor shown, never trusted · resists wash trades and spoofed floors
LTV→ Set per collection by exit math sparse, volatile histories produce low ceilings automatically · max 35% (demo)
Liquidation→ Designed before the first loan committed backstop bids + descending auction with a published clock
Bad debt→ First-class metric on the book screen a lender that hides its bad-debt rate is marketing · 1.1% (demo)

North star: LTV accuracy vs realised sale price. ★

Loan volume is actively perverse here — it grows fastest when underwriting is worst, and adverse selection guarantees the growth arrives. Every other number inherits its honesty from the oracle’s marks being tested against what liquidated collateral actually fetched.

What I got wrong · what’s next

What I got wrong

  • Started with the gallery — a beautiful grid with a borrow button, for a product whose actual product is an oracle.
  • Version one used the raw floor price — one wash trade moved every LTV in the collection; red-teaming my own oracle should have been step one.
  • Modelled liquidity as a constant — the bids that absorb liquidations are correlated with the crash that triggers them.

Next: backtest the oracle against public sale histories and publish the mark-vs-realised error; stress the auction against a simulated cluster of simultaneous liquidations — the scenario the product exists for.
