Coinbase, re-read

Premise

For millions of people, Coinbase is not an exchange; it is what crypto looks like. That makes its retail experience a public utility in a way its product roadmap isn’t — and re-reading that experience as a curious, unconvinced retail user, the friction isn’t missing products. It’s that capable products are hard to find, harder to understand, and impossible to rehearse.

Why it’s hard

An exchange’s revenue gradient points at its most active traders, so the interface drifts toward the people who already trade — density, tickers, order types. The user this POC serves is the opposite persona: they will make five decisions a year, each one anxious. Serving both from one surface is a real product-architecture problem, not a redesign.

The insight

Every addition in this concept is the same move: take something that exists in the market — in several cases, in Coinbase’s own line-up — and rebuild the path to it: discover it in context, understand it in plain language, practise it with nothing at stake. Practice is the second appearance of this site’s core belief: people don’t adopt financial products they can’t rehearse.

What was built

Five surfaces, one persona.

  1. Paper trading — a full practice account with virtual balance and a graduation moment into funded trading. The belief, applied to an exchange.
  2. Learning module — lessons anchored to the surface the user is looking at, not a separate academy tab they’ll never open.
  3. Portfolio analysis — concentration, drawdown and exposure in sentences, not Greek letters. Analysis for the five-decisions-a-year investor.
  4. Derivatives, presented for comprehension — the concept’s framing here is deliberate: US retail futures exist at Coinbase and have since around 2023. What this surface adds is the on-ramp — what leverage means in dollars, rehearsed in the paper account before margin is real.
  5. Events & prediction trading — outcome markets surfaced with the same comprehension-first treatment, because binary questions are how new users actually think about markets.

Success criteria

Volume is the wrong scoreboard for this persona.

The obvious exchange metrics — trading volume, DAU — are maximised by the power users this POC isn’t for; a churning day-trader beats a hundred careful newcomers on both. The north star has to price confidence, not activity.

What I got wrong · what’s next

What I got wrong

  • My first framing was “Coinbase doesn’t have derivatives.” It was factually wrong. Coinbase has offered US futures since around 2023. The error taught me the real shape of the POC: the gap was never the product line — it was that a retail user like my persona couldn’t find, understand or rehearse what already existed. I rebuilt the concept around experience gaps, and it got stronger. Check the claim before you design the fix.
  • I treated prediction markets as a feature. Event contracts sit inside a live regulatory perimeter; shipping them is a licensing and compliance program with a UI on top, not the reverse. The prototype’s cheerful tab understates a year of real work.
  • Portfolio analysis assumed data that doesn’t exist. Useful analysis needs cost basis and holdings across wallets and venues; retail users have neither in one place. The surface demos beautifully on demo data — which is exactly the trap.

What I’d do next: test the graduation mechanic — the paper-to-funded moment — as the single experiment, with the quiz gate on and off; and scope events trading as the compliance program it really is before another pixel is drawn.
